Japan Rail Pass: Complete 2025 Guide

What it costs, what it covers, and an honest route-by-route breakdown of when it saves money — and when it doesn't.

The Japan Rail Pass — universally called the JR Pass — is an unlimited travel pass for foreign tourists covering most of Japan's national rail network, including the Shinkansen bullet trains. For the right itinerary, it's genuinely excellent value. For the wrong one, it's a significant overpay.

That distinction matters more than it used to. In October 2023, JR raised pass prices by nearly 70% — the first increase since 1989. A 7-day Ordinary pass that cost ¥29,650 now costs ¥50,000. The pass is still worth buying for multi-region trips, but the simple Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka round trip that justified it for millions of travellers no longer does. This guide does the maths so you don't have to.

Important: The JR Pass must be purchased before you arrive in Japan, either through the official website or an authorised overseas seller. As of October 2023, it can no longer be purchased at train stations in Japan. You can still buy it inside Japan through select JR Travel Service Centers, but at a significant premium — buy online before you fly.

2025 prices: ordinary vs Green Car

The JR Pass comes in two classes and three durations. The Ordinary pass covers standard-class carriages on all included trains. The Green Car pass covers first-class carriages — more space, quieter, and noticeably more comfortable on longer legs.

USD prices fluctuate with the yen exchange rate — the yen has been historically weak since 2022, which actually makes the pass cheaper in dollar terms than the yen price suggests. Check the current rate when you buy. Children aged 6–11 pay half price; under 6 travels free.

The Green Car is only worth it if you're taking several 2+ hour Shinkansen legs and genuinely value the extra space. For most travellers, the Ordinary pass is the right choice.

What's included — and what's not

The JR Pass covers the national JR network: over 19,000km of rail operated by the six JR Group companies. In practice, that means most of the Shinkansen network plus JR local and express trains in every region. What it doesn't cover is just as important to understand.

Included ✓
  • ✓All Shinkansen lines except Nozomi & Mizuho services
  • ✓Hikari, Sakura, Kodama, Hayabusa, Komachi, Kagayaki bullet trains
  • ✓All JR limited express, express, and local trains nationwide
  • ✓Narita Express (N'EX) — Tokyo airport to city centre
  • ✓Tokyo Monorail to/from Haneda Airport
  • ✓JR ferry to Miyajima (Hiroshima)
  • ✓Some JR bus services
  • ✓Free seat reservations at any JR office
Not included ✗
  • ✗Nozomi & Mizuho Shinkansen (fastest services) — supplement required
  • ✗Tokyo Metro & Toei subway lines
  • ✗Osaka subway system
  • ✗Kyoto city buses and subway
  • ✗Private railways (Kintetsu, Hankyu, Keikyu, etc.)
  • ✗Hakone Romancecar and ropeway
  • ✗Most services to/from Osaka Itami Airport
  • ✗Residents of Japan (temporary visitor visa only)

The Nozomi and Mizuho exclusion is worth flagging. These are the fastest Shinkansen services — Tokyo to Kyoto on the Nozomi takes 2h13m; the Hikari (which is covered) takes 2h40m. The 27-minute difference rarely matters for sightseeing itineraries. However, if you want to ride the Nozomi, you can now pay a supplement as a JR Pass holder — around ¥4,000–¥6,500 per trip.

Subways and city buses in Tokyo, Osaka, and Kyoto are not covered. You'll need a Suica or PASMO IC card for those regardless of whether you have a JR Pass — load it with ¥3,000–¥5,000 when you arrive and top up as needed.

How to buy and activate your JR Pass

1
Buy online before you leave home

Purchase from the official Japan Rail Pass website (japanrailpass.net) or an authorised overseas seller. Choose your pass type and duration, select your intended activation date (you can change this later within a 30-day window), and pay online. You'll receive an exchange order by email.

2
Exchange your order at a JR office in Japan

On arrival, take your exchange order and your passport to a JR Travel Service Center — found at all major airports (Narita, Haneda, Kansai) and large stations. Bring your original passport; a photocopy won't work. The officer will confirm your temporary visitor stamp. Allow 15–30 minutes; it can take longer if the office is busy.

3
Choose your activation date carefully

You don't have to activate the pass the day you exchange it. If you're spending a few days in Tokyo before your Shinkansen travel begins, delay activation until your first long-distance trip. A 7-day pass activated in Tokyo 3 days into your trip gives you 7 full days for the travel-intensive part of your itinerary.

4
Reserve your Shinkansen seats at the same time

Seat reservations are free and unlimited for JR Pass holders. Do them all at once when you exchange your pass — bring a printed or screenshot itinerary. The officer will book each leg. You can also reserve at JR ticket machines using the pass itself, or at any Green Window (Midori no Madoguchi) counter.

5
Use the dedicated JR Pass gates

At Shinkansen ticket gates, don't try to tap through the IC card turnstiles — you'll be blocked. Find the staffed gate (usually on the left or right of the main barriers) and show your pass to the attendant. They'll wave you through. On the train, keep your pass accessible — conductors do occasional checks.

Regional passes: often the smarter choice

If your itinerary stays within one part of Japan, a regional JR pass will almost always save you money over the national JR Pass. There are over 20 regional options. Here are the most useful for common itineraries:

JR Kansai Area Pass
From ¥2,400 (1 day) · ¥4,000 (2 days)
Covers Osaka, Kyoto, Nara, Kobe, Himeji, and Wakayama on JR lines. Ideal if you're spending a week in Kansai without going to Tokyo or Hiroshima.
JR Kansai Wide Area Pass
¥12,000 (5 days)
Extends Kansai pass coverage to include Kinosaki Onsen, Hiroshima via the San'yo line, Okayama, and the Haruka airport express from Kansai Airport. Excellent value for a Kansai-centred trip with a Hiroshima day trip.
JR East Pass (Tohoku)
¥20,000 (5 days in 14)
Covers all JR East lines north of Tokyo — Tohoku Shinkansen to Sendai, Aomori, and connections to Hokkaido. Ideal for Tokyo + northern Japan itineraries.
Hokuriku Arch Pass
¥30,000 (7 days)
Connects Tokyo to Osaka via the scenic Hokuriku route through Kanazawa and Fukui — the same cities as the national JR Pass, but cheaper. Best for Tokyo + Kanazawa + Kyoto trips.
JR Kyushu Rail Pass
¥9,000 (3 days) · ¥14,000 (5 days)
Unlimited JR travel within Kyushu — Fukuoka, Nagasaki, Kagoshima, and beyond. Much better value than the national pass if Kyushu is your sole destination.
JR Tokyo Wide Pass
¥15,000 (3 days)
Covers day trips from Tokyo in all directions — Nikko, Hakone (JR portion), Kawaguchiko, Nagano, Karuizawa. Good value if you're Tokyo-based and doing multiple day trips.

The key principle: if your entire trip stays within one JR regional company's network, buy that company's pass. If you're crossing two or more regions on long-distance Shinkansen, run the numbers on the national pass.

Tips for getting the most out of your JR Pass

  • Use it for the N'EX from Narita. The Narita Express from the airport to central Tokyo costs ¥3,070 one-way. If you have a JR Pass, this journey is free — one of the easiest ways to extract immediate value. Do this on arrival day even if you're not activating the pass for inter-city travel yet.
  • Reserve every Shinkansen seat in advance. While unreserved cars exist, the number of unreserved seats has been shrinking on many routes. During peak periods (Golden Week, Obon, New Year), unreserved cars can be standing-room only. Reservations are free — always book them.
  • Don't activate during Golden Week or Obon. Popular trains book out entirely during Japan's two main holiday periods (late April/early May and mid-August). Your free reservations won't help if no seats are available. Aim to start your travel-intensive week just before or just after these windows.
  • Buy a Suica card at the airport regardless. The JR Pass doesn't work on subways, city buses, or private railways. A Suica (IC card) handles all of that and works at convenience stores too. Load ¥3,000–¥5,000 on arrival.
  • The Hikari vs Nozomi difference is usually fine. The Nozomi is 20–30 minutes faster than the Hikari on Tokyo–Kyoto. For sightseeing, that time is irrelevant. Don't pay a supplement just to save half an hour.
  • Day trips are the pass's hidden value. Once you have a JR Pass, the marginal cost of a day trip is zero. This encourages exploring beyond the main cities — Himeji Castle from Osaka, Hiroshima and Miyajima from Kyoto, Kamakura from Tokyo. These trips would cost ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person without a pass and become free with one.

Frequently asked questions

JR Pass vs Japan Rail Pass — is there a difference?

None at all. JR Pass is shorthand for Japan Rail Pass. Both terms refer to the same product. "JR" stands for Japan Railways (the JR Group), the company that operates the network.

Can I buy the JR Pass inside Japan?

Technically yes — since 2023 you can buy it at select JR offices inside Japan, but at a roughly 10% premium over the online price. Always buy it before you arrive.

What visa do I need to qualify?

You must be entering Japan as a temporary visitor — the standard 90-day tourist visa that most nationalities receive on arrival. Long-stay visas, work visas, and Japanese residency all disqualify you. Your passport will be checked when you exchange your pass.

Can I use the JR Pass on the Tokyo Metro?

No. The Tokyo Metro is a private operator, not part of the JR Group. You'll need a Suica or PASMO card for subway travel in Tokyo. The JR Yamanote Line (which circles central Tokyo) is covered, but the subway lines below ground are not.

Does the JR Pass cover the bullet train to Hokkaido?

Yes — the Hokkaido Shinkansen from Shin-Aomori to Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto is covered. However, the full journey from Tokyo to Sapporo via Shinkansen takes around 5 hours and requires a change. If Hokkaido is a core part of your itinerary, check whether the JR East–South Hokkaido Pass offers better value.

Is the Green Car worth the extra cost?

For most people, no. The Ordinary class on Japan's Shinkansen is already comfortable, clean, and has good legroom. The Green Car offers wider seats, more recline, and quieter carriages — nice for a 4+ hour journey, but hard to justify the ¥20,000–¥40,000 premium over the Ordinary pass.

What happens if I lose my JR Pass?

Lost or stolen passes cannot be replaced. Keep a photo of the pass number and your exchange order confirmation — these may help with insurance claims, but JR will not issue a replacement. Treat it like cash.
