Amtrak Coast Starlight: The Complete 2025 Route Guide

Los Angeles to Seattle along the Pacific Coast — stops, schedules, tickets, the best seats for ocean views, and what the journey is actually like.

The Coast Starlight is Amtrak's great coastal journey — 1,377 miles of Pacific shoreline, volcanic peaks, and old-growth forest between Los Angeles and Seattle. It's the only Amtrak route that traces the entire length of the West Coast, and it does it in a single overnight trip that takes just one night away from you.

Unlike the California Zephyr's 51-hour marathon, the Starlight clocks in at around 35 hours — short enough to do in coach if you're budget-conscious, but long enough to justify a sleeper room if you want the full experience. The train runs daily in both directions, and the stretch between Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo — where the tracks run literally at the edge of the Pacific — is among the most dramatic rail scenery in North America.

At a glance

Route: Los Angeles Union Station → Seattle King Street Station (or reverse)
Distance: 1,377 miles  ·  Duration: ~35 hours southbound, ~34 hours northbound

The best scenic stretches

The Coast Starlight has four distinct scenic acts, each worth being awake for. In the southbound direction, here's when and where to be in the Sightseer Lounge:

Washington
Puget Sound & Cascade foothills
Departing Seattle, the train skirts the eastern shore of Puget Sound before climbing into dense Pacific Northwest forest. Low cloud and fog give it an atmospheric, moody quality — especially in the mornings.
Southbound Day 1, 10 AM–1 PM
Oregon / Northern California
Mount Shasta & the Cascades
The volcanic cone of Mount Shasta dominates the skyline for nearly two hours as the train descends through the Cascades into California. Northbound riders catch this at sunrise — one of the best moments on the whole route.
Southbound: early morning Day 2, ~3–5 AM (bring night-vision or sleep through it northbound)
Central California
The Pacific Coast stretch
The undisputed highlight. Between San Luis Obispo and Oxnard, the tracks run within metres of the water along cliffs and beaches. This is where the Coast Starlight earns its name — the train hugs the shoreline so closely you can hear the waves if you open a window.
Southbound Day 2, ~3–6 PM — perfect golden-hour light
Northern California
Sacramento Valley & the Bay
The approach to the San Francisco Bay through Sacramento and the East Bay is more urban but still pleasant, with views of the bay and the hills of the Marin Headlands in the distance as you pull into Emeryville.
Southbound Day 2, ~9–11 AM

Pro tip: If you can only ride one segment, do the coastal stretch southbound in the afternoon. Northbound riders miss most of it in the dark. The timetable has the train departing LA in the morning northbound, meaning you hit the Santa Barbara coast around midday — also excellent.

Best seats for ocean views

This is the question every Coast Starlight traveller asks, and the honest answer is more nuanced than most guides admit. Here's the definitive breakdown:

🪟
Ocean side seating
Sit on the right side heading south, left side heading north
The Pacific Ocean is to the west. Heading southbound (Seattle → LA), the ocean is on your right. Heading northbound (LA → Seattle), the ocean is on your left. For coach seats, ask for the ocean-facing side when you board. The Sightseer Lounge car has windows on both sides and wrap-around views — the best bet regardless of which seat you're assigned.

For roomette passengers: the car orientation is set in the train yard and you won't know which way it's facing until you board. Odd-numbered roomettes are typically on the ocean side when the car is in standard orientation, but this is not guaranteed — cars are occasionally reversed. Your best strategy is to spend the Santa Barbara–SLO stretch in the Sightseer Lounge regardless of your room.

For Mount Shasta views (northbound), you want the east-facing windows — the right side heading north. Again, the Sightseer Lounge handles this automatically since it wraps around.

How to book and save

The Coast Starlight uses Amtrak's dynamic pricing, meaning fares rise as seats fill. A few strategies that consistently work:

  • Book 2–3 months ahead for roomettes. Summer and autumn are peak seasons — July and August departures sell out the furthest in advance. Weekday departures (Tuesday/Wednesday) are usually 10–20% cheaper than weekends.
  • The Los Angeles → Seattle direction is often cheaper than the reverse. Check both directions if your itinerary is flexible.
  • Partial-route segments are strong value. LA → Santa Barbara (under 3 hours) or Emeryville → Portland (an overnight) give you the scenic highlights without committing to the full 35 hours.
  • Students save 15% with code V814. The national student discount applies to the Coast Starlight for coach seats. See our Amtrak student discount guide for full details.
  • Join Amtrak Guest Rewards before you book. Points accrue on every purchase, including discounted fares.

Practical tips for the journey

  • Get to the Sightseer Lounge early for the coastal stretch. Between Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo the lounge fills completely. Stake a window spot at least 30 minutes before Oxnard if you're heading north, or before San Luis Obispo heading south.
  • The Sightseer Lounge has a lower level café with sandwiches, snacks, and drinks. It's open to all passengers. Hot food in the dining car upstairs is table-service for sleeper passengers at set meal times.
  • Cell service disappears for long stretches, especially through Oregon and the Cascades. Download entertainment before you board. The train has wifi but it's unreliable — treat it as a bonus, not a given.
  • Power outlets are at every coach seat and in all sleeping car rooms. Bring a multi-plug adaptor if you're travelling with multiple devices.
  • For the northbound Mount Shasta view, set an alarm for around 5–6 AM — the mountain appears before the sun is fully up. Clear mornings give you a snow-capped volcanic cone against a pale blue sky. Worth the early wake-up.
  • San Francisco is not a direct stop. Emeryville is the closest station. Amtrak runs a free connecting bus from Emeryville to the Caltrain station and the Ferry Building in San Francisco — it's included in your ticket and takes about 30 minutes.
  • The Portland stop is around 45 minutes — long enough to get off and stretch your legs or grab food from the station food court. Don't wander far; the train leaves on time.
  • Pack layers. The train's air conditioning is aggressively cold, especially overnight in coach. Bring a blanket or a light jacket even in summer.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Coast Starlight worth taking?

Yes, especially for the southern California coastal section. The stretch between Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo is genuinely extraordinary — the train runs so close to the ocean you could almost reach out and touch the water. If you're already travelling the West Coast and have a day to spare, it beats flying for the experience.

How late does the Coast Starlight run?

Frequently. The train averages around 1–2 hours late on most runs, and can run significantly more on bad days. This is a structural issue with freight-priority rail in the US, not something Amtrak can easily fix. Plan accordingly — don't book a connecting flight within 3 hours of your scheduled arrival, and check the real-time status on departure day.

Do I need a sleeper for the overnight portion?

Not necessarily. The coast from Santa Barbara to SLO is entirely in daylight on both southbound and northbound runs, so the key scenic section doesn't require an overnight stay. If you're on a tight budget, coach is manageable for the full trip — the seats recline significantly and the lounge car gives you somewhere to sit upright. That said, a roomette transforms the experience for what is still a reasonable price premium.

Can I do just part of the route?

Absolutely — Amtrak sells tickets for any segment. Popular partial routes include LA → Santa Barbara (2.5 hours, beautiful coastal day trip), Emeryville → Portland (overnight, catches the Cascades and Sacramento), and Seattle → Portland (4.5 hours, a scenic afternoon run through Washington). Point-to-point pricing means shorter segments are generally cheaper than you'd expect.

Is the dining car still running?

Yes. As of 2025, the Coast Starlight has a traditional full-service dining car with table service for sleeper passengers. Coach passengers use the Sightseer Lounge café for purchases. The dining car reopened on long-distance trains after a pandemic-era hiatus; the Coast Starlight was one of the first to restore it fully.

How do I get from Emeryville to San Francisco?

Amtrak provides a free connecting bus (the Amtrak Thruway Bus) from Emeryville station to the SF Ferry Building. It's included in your ticket and runs automatically when the train arrives. The journey takes about 20–30 minutes depending on Bay Bridge traffic. No separate booking required — just board the bus with your Amtrak ticket.
